Saltmere

A fisherman pulls you out of the sea on the isle of Caldra. You remember nothing. That night goblins come over the fence, and you fight them off. Now you are Saltmere's hero: go out and fight, bring home what you find, and grow a village that feeds, houses and defends itself.

3 classes
65 buildings to order
54 village jobs
26 × 17 km island
Free, no ads, plays offline

Getting started

The opening walks you through the basics, one goal at a time (the goal is always at the top of the screen):

  • Talk to Bram the fisherman, who found you in his nets on the beach.
  • Find Elder Maren in the longhouse up the path.
  • Walk Tomas the woodcutter to the forest outside the north gate and keep the wolves off him.
  • Supper at the Salty Gull, the tavern, with the whole village.
  • Goblins! The first raid comes that night. Defend Saltmere.
  • Order a watchtower from the village plan. From then on the island is yours to explore.

Controls (phone)

  • The joystick (left) walks; Attack aims itself at the nearest foe; four skill buttons; a Potion button.
  • The action button (E on a keyboard) talks, searches, trades, opens doors and trashes goblin camps.
  • Village plan switches to the bird's-eye view: tap a building, then where it should go. Tap a building or a villager for their card.
  • Goods and mood shows the stores, the mood, the people, and the ledger of what was made and eaten yesterday. Map shows where you've been.
  • The game saves by itself every five minutes and when you come home; Save saves now.

Day and night

A day lasts 8 minutes. Night is 21:00 to 05:00: people sleep, and raiders come. Villagers work from 7:00 to 18:00, with meals around 7:00, 12:00 and 18:00, and spend evenings at the tavern.

If you fall

The villagers carry you to the healer's hut, and you wake up there. The village loses a tenth of its gold.

Spoiler warning. This gives away who the hero really is. It's more fun to find out in the game. Show it anyway?

The truth comes back piece by piece as you lift the island's curses:

  • You are the last Warden of the Veil, of an old order sworn to keep the Hollow shut: the grey realm between death and rest.
  • Three years ago the order fell at Veilwatch in one night. Its own High Warden, Veyra, betrayed it.
  • You were Veyra's second and her apprentice. You carry the Veil Key, the last seal on the Hollow, bound into your blood.
  • Your ship, the Grey Gull, carried the last Wardens south. Veyra's storm sank it.
  • The Forgetting, Veyra's curse, took your memory so you would not remember how to stop her.
  • That is why goblins grow bolder near you, the dead stir in the barrows, and the sea gives back drowned men on moonless nights. The Hollow is calling the Key.

Your class is the kind of Warden you were: a Shield of the Veil (warrior), a Lantern-bearer (wizard) or a Speaker (summoner), who talks with the dead and the wild.

Kill monsters for experience. Every level gives a skill point. Levels need 40, 110, 210, 340, 500, 700… experience in total.

The village

Saltmere starts with twelve people: two fishers, two woodcutters, the elder, the cook, the healer, two builders, a farmer with no farm yet, and two looking for work. Everyone has a name, a job, hunger, thirst and health.

Work and priorities

  • The village gives out the work by itself: people without a job take whatever needs hands, food first.
  • Every building with workers has a work priority: Paused, Low, Normal or High. When food runs low, food buildings count one step higher.
  • The Workers window (the button by Goods and Map) works like Timberborn's: at the top, how many people are at work, building or have nothing to do, and how many places stand empty; then a row for every job, with workers / wanted and how many places it has. The − and + buttons change how many workers a job wants, and the arrow opens its workplaces one by one, each with its own − and +, and its priority (Paused, Normal or High). Want fewer at a workplace and the rest go to other work; want more and free hands (or people from less important work) come.
  • Bring a worker here (on a building's card) fetches someone from the least important work. They stay there: Stays at this job on a person's card keeps the village from moving them.
  • Builders fetch materials from the stores and put up whatever you order, the oldest order first.

Work speed and mood

All work goes at 75% to 125% speed, by the village's mood (0 to 100). The mood starts from 45 and moves towards:

  • + services (tavern, chapel, bard, inn…) up to +30; + varied food up to +10; + ale or wine at the tavern +6; + warm clothes for everyone +4
  • − 4 for each person without a bed; − 3 for each person hungry or thirsty; − 8 while the village grieves a death

Newcomers settle when there are free beds, the mood is 45 or better, and there's food for everyone. An inn and a scout lodge bring more.

What a building should make, and who makes it

Every building that makes something shows its Expected output on its card, next to what it really made yesterday, for example:

Expected: about 12 fish a day: Bram and Ylva, 1 fish per 1.2 h each at 100% work speed, and 1.4 h to carry each basket to the stores and come back

It's worked out like this. Workers really work about 9 hours a day (7:00 to 18:00, less meals, drinks and walks):

batches a day = workers × 9 h × work speed ÷ (hours a batch + work speed × walk to the stores ÷ batches a trip)

Only fishers, farmers and woodcutters carry what they make to the stores. Fishers and farmers fill a basket of 4 first. Everyone else puts it straight in the stores. When a building makes nothing, the card says why: no workers, paused, waiting for grain, the stores are full, burned by raiders.

Tools

Every worker carries their own tools, and works faster with them. They fetch the best tools in the stores by themselves; tools wear out with work, and then they fetch new ones. Workers at more important work (a higher priority) get the better tools first. A person's card shows their tools and the hours left; the Goods and mood window shows how many workers have none.

Porters

Build a Porters' yard and its porters take over the carrying: fishers, farmers, woodcutters and hunters leave what they make in a crate at their workplace (on the pier, by the field, at the lodge) and go straight back to work, and the porters carry the crates to the stores. With the crates empty they fetch the builders' materials. A porter on foot carries 6 a trip; a handcart (carpenter: 4 planks, 1 iron tools) carries 15, an ox cart (stables: a handcart, plough oxen and 6 grain) carries 30. The yard's card shows how much they should carry a day, and each building's card what's waiting in its crate.

Storehouses

The longhouse, storehouses and warehouses hold four kinds of goods: food, water, materials (wood, stone, ore, clay, hides…) and goods (planks, tools, cloth, ale…). Tap one and switch the kinds it takes on and off, for example a storehouse by the pier for food only. People carry things to the nearest store that takes them, and eat and drink at stores that take food and water. The Goods and mood window shows the room left for each kind. When there's no room for fish, the fishers have nowhere to put their catch, and their card says so.

Hurt and downed villagers

A villager at zero health is down: you have 30 seconds to reach them and stand by them to help them up (a red arrow points the way). Otherwise they're carried to the healer, badly hurt, for a day. With no food or water for too long, people die.

Food and water

  • Hunger runs out in 36 hours, thirst in 24. People eat and drink at mealtimes, and whenever they get low.
  • A person uses about 66.7 hunger a day: about 1 hot meal, or 1.5 fish. The cook at the Salty Gull turns fish and grain into meals one for one, so cooking makes food go about half as far again.
  • Each person drinks 3 buckets a day. A well holds 40 and refills 2 an hour (48 a day): one well for about 15 people. The water carrier takes the extra to the stores.
  • The Goods and mood window shows the expected food a day against what the village needs, and yesterday's food made and eaten, by building.

Gear

Monsters, camps, chests and landmarks drop gear. Wear it, sell it (at a market, or to the Gilded Wheel's caravan when it comes), salvage it at a smithy, or donate it to the armoury: guards and soldiers come first and take whatever beats what they wear; then working villagers (those outside the walls first) take light gear: a sword, axe or dagger, a hood, tunic or robe, gloves and boots, nothing legendary. An armed villager stands and fights one or two beasts, a wolf, a lynx, rats, instead of running (never a raid or a boss), then picks up whatever it dropped (hide, meat, gold, gear) and carries it to the stores; gear goes to the armoury. (Potions stay on the ground for you.) Tap a piece (in your bag or on the caravan's table) to see its stats, and stat by stat what it would change against what you wear.

Raids and monsters

Goblin camps

Up to four goblin camps grow out on the island, a stage about every day. Kill a camp's goblins and trash it (the action button) to get its stolen goods, gold and gear; a new camp turns up somewhere else a day or two later.

StageWhat's there
Scout camp2 goblins, two tents
Goblin camp3 goblins and a totem
Fortified camp5 goblins behind stakes
War camp6 goblins under banners
Warband8 goblins and war drums: it marches on Saltmere the next night (unless Saltmere was attacked in the last two or three days: then it waits)

A lookout tower (14 wood, 2 stone) shows you where the danger is: its lookout watches everything within 450 m from the top (wolves, boars and goblins show on your map), and every few hours sends out quick runners who report every goblin camp within 1.2 km, the whole area where camps grow, with how far each has grown. The tower's card lists what's out there, nearest first.

A warband attacks at night. War drums sound about 19:00 (scouts or a lighthouse warn you earlier), and the raid comes at 21:30. Raids grow bigger every day. A camp takes about a day and a half (28 to 46 hours) to grow each stage, so a new camp is a warband after about six days; there are at most three camps at a time, a trashed camp is replaced after 36 to 72 hours, and after any attack on Saltmere (goblins or rats) there is a quiet spell of two to three days (40 to 76 hours) before the next warband marches. Watchtowers with guards, palisade walls, gates, barracks and an archery range all help.

Getting about

The island is big: about 45 minutes on foot from Saltmere to Stenhamn. Two services carry you between places you've already been to. Stand at a stop and press the action button (E) to see where you can go, the fare (paid from Saltmere's stores) and the hours on the way. The trip is a short scene: the hours pass (the camps grow, the wells fill) and you step off at the other end.

  • The Gilded Wheel's post-horses run between its waystations: Askeby and Stenhamn have one, and Saltmere builds its own (Village tab: 24 wood, 6 stone). To ride home from a town, Saltmere needs its own waystation. About 12 km an hour along the roads; about 3 gold plus 2 a km.
  • The Tidewrights' boat sails along the coast between Saltmere's pier, Gullbo, Stenhamn's harbour and Hornvik. About 9 km an hour; about 2 gold plus 1.5 a km.
  • A stop opens the first time you reach it on foot. Not with monsters about, and not while Saltmere is under attack.

Exploring

  • The Old Barrow, a burial mound by the road west of the crossroads: a crypt full of risen skeletons, with its king and his treasure at the far end. Once he's beaten, the dead rest for three days.
  • Landmarks to find, most with something to search once: a ruined watchtower, a hunter's lean-to and the Fallen Giant in the Old Forest; a shipwreck on the eastern beach and an old lighthouse on the western headland; a ring of stones and an abandoned farmstead in the Green Hills; the witch's hut in Mirefen; the Great Oak and elven ruins round a moonwell in Elderwood; waystones and shrines along the roads.
  • Askeby, the market town on the King's Road (about 3.7 km east), and Stenhamn, the capital on the east coast, are real towns: a wall round each (a timber palisade, at Stenhamn grey stone with towers) with a gate on every road, the roads straight through to a paved square with a fountain, the houses round it between the streets, an inn and a market; the Gilded Wheel's waystation stands just inside the gate on the Saltmere road (look for its banner, a gold wheel on crimson; it's on your map too). Every faction flies its own banner: the kingdom a gold crown on royal blue (on Stenhamn's gatehouse and the palace), the Tidewrights a ship over waves (by each pier), Askeby an ash tree, Gullbo a gull, Hornvik a hunting horn, Saltmere a herring, and the other peoples theirs where their banners stand (a raven for Ravnsund, a crescent moon for the elves, an anvil for the dwarves, a round door for the halflings, a flame for the orcs, and so on). Stenhamn has a harbour, the guild hall and the Queen's palace. Gullbo and Hornvik are small harbours where the boat calls. The other peoples' homes are still banners: their towns come after 1.0.
  • Hunting: deer and stags bolt, boars fight back when hurt. Their kills give hide and meat.

Rulers and peoples

The House of Varr

  • King Aldric Varr drowned a year ago when his barge sank on a calm lake. No one believes it was an accident.
  • Queen Isolde Varr, 22, his daughter: clever and stubborn, untested, crowned in a hurry.
  • Duke Corvin Varr, Aldric's younger brother, beloved by the army, and sure the crown should be his.
  • Rafe, "the Magpie", who leads the Velvet Hand thieves in Hornvik.
  • Lady Brenna Varr, Corvin's daughter.

The other peoples

  • Moonmother Sylwen rules the elves of Lirael, whose borders are closed to humans since soldiers burned the eastern Elderwood sixty years ago.
  • High Thane Brakka Stonehand rules the dwarves of Durnhold, in the mountain.
  • Khan Varrok Ashmane leads the orc clans of Ashkar. His father Torgal was murdered at the Ember Pass, and the orcs blame the dwarves.
  • Burrowmother Tansy Applegate leads the halflings of Hollowbrook, the island's breadbasket.
  • Chief Grumm Mossback leads the bog trolls of Gnarlroot, in Mirefen.

Saltmere's own

Bram the fisherman found you. Elder Maren runs the village. Tomas is the woodcutter you escorted. Hedda cooks at the Salty Gull. Signe is the healer.

  • Duke Corvin arranged his brother's drowning with a curse bought from Veyra, who promised him the crown. He does not know what she wants in return.

The story

The crown war between Queen Isolde in Stenhamn and Duke Corvin in Ravnsund splits the realm. Dwarves and orcs feud over the Ember Pass, elves keep their borders shut, mages and priests quarrel over magic, and the halflings starve in the middle. And the goblin camps keep growing until they march on Saltmere.

Who you are, why the curses are spreading, and how it all ends is for you to find out. Chapter One (version 1.0) takes you from the beach to the capital.

Spoiler warning. This gives away how Chapter One goes, step by step. It's more fun to find out in the game. Show it anyway?

  • The morning after the watchtower is ordered, Elder Maren sends for you: the raids grow too fast to be natural, and the Queen must hear it from someone who has fought them. She tells you to show the Queen the mark on your arm.
  • Follow the King's Road east from the crossroads to Askeby and talk to Reeve Halvard: goblins have been seen on the road east of Hollowbrook.
  • On the way to Stenhamn, goblins ambush you on the King's Road, far from their camps.
  • In Stenhamn, Queen Isolde receives you before her palace. She knows the mark: a Warden of the Veil's. A memory comes back: a tower in the snow, a broken gate, a woman calling your name. Chapter One is complete (+150 gold, +200 experience).

  • The crown. Back the Queen: Tidecutter and the Velvet Hand, but the Iron Oath splits and Ravnsund burns. Back the Duke: the Iron Oath's armies, but you have helped a murderer. Or unite them through Lady Brenna, the hardest path: Corvin abdicates and the realm stays whole.
  • The Ember Pass. Expose the Duke's lie, and dwarves and orcs make peace and march together.
  • Elderwood. Reconcile Caelith and the elves join you; kill him and they join, but never forgive humans.
  • The Hag. Bargain with her and she helps against Veyra, at a price you learn later.

Every people and guild you win over joins the Lantern Pact. The more who stand at Veilwatch for the last battle, the more endings open:

The Mended VeilYou seal the Hollow with the Key. Veyra is lost in it with her daughter's shade, at peace at last.
The Open DoorYou let Veyra take Liesl back. The Hollow stays open, and the dead walk with the living forever.
The Last WardenYou take Veyra's place, and become what she was.
The Pale DragonFreed, Ysmirr carries you to the last battle. Slain, its heart forges the final seal.
